## What shipped

Three process-lifecycle flags on `scan`, for supervisors running many
concurrent scans across worktrees: `--spool-dir` (with a lock-based reaper for
spools no live scan owns), `--progress-file` (append-only JSONL heartbeat,
contract in `docs/contracts/progress-file-v1.md`), and `--parent-pid` (the
extractor self-terminates when its supervisor dies; Unix-only, accepted and
ignored elsewhere). All three are opt-in — an argv without them is byte-identical
to 2.21.0's behavior.

## Cross-model review before the merge

Two independent adversarial reviews (Codex `gpt-5.1-codex-max`, Grok
`grok-4.5`) produced 12 findings. The three both models reached independently
were the three that were real; the full triage, including the refutations, is
`docs/reviews/2026-08-03-fleet-safety-flags-cross-model-review.md`.

- **Critical — the progress file was truncated before its identity was proven.**
  The path-based collision guard ran, then `File::create` truncated on open, so
  `--progress-file` pointed at a hard link to the artifact destroyed it before
  the second guard could refuse. Fixed by opening without truncating, proving
  identity on the open handle via `same-file`, and only then `set_len(0)`
  (decision 8b). Proven against a real 1,110,016-byte artifact: refused
  `invalid_path`, artifact byte-identical afterwards.
- **High — the reaper released the sentinel lock before unlinking the spool.**
  `claim_sentinel` now returns the locked handle and the claim outlives the
  unlink (decision 11a).
- **Doc — `cli.md` contradicted itself on Windows identity**, left over from the
  same-file change that closed the NTFS hard-link gap.

Mutation-proved: restoring truncate-on-open and unlock-before-unlink fails three
tests; reverting returns 167 passed.
